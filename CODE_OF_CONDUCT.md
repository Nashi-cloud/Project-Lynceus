# Code of conduct

**English** · [Français](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.fr.md)

This project builds a tool that describes methods rather than judging people. The same rule
applies to the people who build it, and to the people who come to talk about it.

Written in the project's own words rather than imported wholesale, because a text nobody reads
enforces nothing. It covers the same ground as the Contributor Covenant, whose structure it
follows, and it is deliberately shorter.

## Where it applies

Every space the project holds: issues, pull requests, commit messages, discussions, and any
place where someone speaks in the name of Lynceus. It also applies to what happens outside
those spaces when the target is a person because of their involvement here.

## What is expected

Assume competence and good faith in the person you are answering. Criticise the work, name
what is wrong with it, and say what would be better; that is the whole job of a review.
Accept that the answer may be no, and that it may come with a reason you disagree with.

Say when you are unsure. A project that measures its own error rate in public cannot ask its
contributors to pretend certainty.

## What is not accepted

Harassment, in public or in private. Sexual, racist, sexist, ableist or otherwise degrading
remarks. Attacks on a person rather than on their work. Publishing someone's private
information without their explicit consent. Sustained disruption of a discussion. Unwanted
attention after being asked to stop.

Being right does not make any of this acceptable, and neither does being a maintainer.

## An analysis you disagree with is not a conduct matter

The tool grades pages, and some of those grades will be low. If you publish a page Lynceus
graded badly, or if you simply think an analysis is wrong, that goes through the **dispute
channel** on any card, not through an issue and not through this document. A dispute is
recorded, counted, and handed to the operator of the instance, whose decision is kept.

Anger at an analysis is legitimate. Directing it at a person is what this document is about.

## Reporting

Write to **raphael@nashi.cloud**. Reports are read by the maintainer of the project.

Say what happened, where, and when. A link is enough if the exchange is public. You will get
an answer. What you send is not shared beyond what handling it requires, and never with the
person reported without asking you first.

Reporting in good faith costs you nothing here, including when the person you are reporting is
the maintainer. If that is the case and you would rather not write to him, say so in a public
issue in whatever terms you find bearable, or use GitHub's own reporting.

## What follows

Depending on what happened, and in this order: a private word, a public correction, the
removal of a message, a temporary exclusion from the project's spaces, a permanent one. A
first response is rarely the last step of that list, and a serious enough act goes straight to
it.

The reason for a decision is given to the person concerned. The project is run by one person
today, which makes this a promise rather than a procedure, and it is stated as such.
