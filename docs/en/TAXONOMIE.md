# Catalogue of techniques — the Lynceus reference list

> Translation for information. The French version, `docs/TAXONOMIE.md`, is the list the
> engine applies, and the only one injected into the prompt: should the two ever diverge,
> it is the one that counts. The `ids` never change, in any language.

Version: **0.1.7** — 31 techniques. This catalogue is the **closed list** of detectable techniques: every detection in an analysis card must carry an `id` from this list (validated by the server) and a verbatim excerpt from the page.

Each entry: definition, detection cues, psychological mechanism. Aligned with the **FLICC** taxonomy (John Cook), the *Debunking Handbook 2020*, and the work on inoculation (van der Linden). The severity given is *indicative*: context prevails.

---

## Family A — Emotional register

### `appel_a_la_peur` — Appeal to fear · high severity
Raising fear or threat so that a conclusion is accepted without examination.
**Cues:** catastrophist vocabulary, imminent danger, “before it is too late”, terrible consequences with no source.
**Mechanism:** strong emotion reduces analytical vigilance and pushes towards immediate action (sharing, buying, joining).

### `urgence_artificielle` — Manufactured urgency · medium severity
Creating unjustified time pressure.
**Cues:** “act now”, countdowns, “soon censored or banned”, limited stock.
**Mechanism:** urgency short-circuits deliberation, a technique shared by commercial scams and propaganda alike.

### `appel_a_l_indignation` — Appeal to outrage · medium severity
Provoking anger to bind the reader against a target and prompt them to share.
**Cues:** “outrageous”, “shameful”, direct address (“and you think that is normal?!”), a designated target.
**Mechanism:** anger is the most viral emotion; it turns the reader into a campaigning relay.

## Family B — Rhetoric of secrecy and conspiracy

### `verite_cachee` — Hidden-truth rhetoric · high severity
Presenting the information as a secret that “they” are concealing.
**Cues:** “what they are hiding from you”, “the media will not cover this”, “censored information”, “them”.
**Mechanism:** flatters the reader (one of the awakened) and immunises the content against refutation, since any criticism “proves” the plot.

### `eux_contre_nous` — “Us against them” framing · high severity
Splitting the world into a clear-sighted “us” and a malicious “them” (elites, the system, Big Pharma and so on).
**Cues:** vague, homogeneous entities (“the elites”, “the system”), intent ascribed to an entire group.
**Mechanism:** belonging to the group matters more than accuracy; leaving the belief means betraying the group.

### `hyper_intentionnalisme` — Nothing happens by chance · high severity
Ascribing coordinated intent to every event; ruling out chance, error and incompetence.
**Cues:** “coincidence? I think not”, disparate events reread as a single plan.
**Mechanism:** the brain prefers an intentional story to chaos; a plot is more reassuring than randomness (proportionality bias).

### `millefeuille_argumentatif` — Argument stacking · medium severity
Piling up many weak arguments to give an impression of overwhelming proof.
**Cues:** endless lists of assorted “evidence”, none solid on its own; “there is too much for it to be false”.
**Mechanism:** quantity simulates quality; refuting each point is expensive (Brandolini's asymmetry).

### `heros_persecute` — The persecuted hero · medium severity
Presenting the censorship or persecution of the author (real or supposed) as proof of truthfulness.
**Cues:** “they want to silence me”, “video removed X times”, media martyrdom, “they are afraid of the truth”.
**Mechanism:** reverses the burden of proof, so that opposition encountered becomes the argument. Galileo is summoned; his method is not.

## Family C — Pseudo-reasoning

### `cherry_picking` — Cherry picking · high severity
Keeping only the data that suits the thesis, ignoring the rest.
**Cues:** an isolated study against a consensus, a conveniently chosen year or country, an anomaly turned into a rule.
**Mechanism:** every element quoted is true; it is the selection that lies. Very hard to spot without the whole picture.

### `correlation_causation` — Correlation mistaken for causation · medium severity
Inferring cause and effect from a mere coincidence in time or statistics.
**Cues:** “since X, Y has risen”, superimposed charts, “right after…”.
**Mechanism:** the brain's natural causal intuition; ignoring common factors and chance is easier.

### `faux_dilemme` — False dilemma · medium severity
Reducing a complex problem to two options, only one of which is acceptable.
**Cues:** “either… or”, “those who are not with us”, deliberate absence of intermediate positions.
**Mechanism:** simplifies the decision and enlists: refusing option A means “choosing” option B.

### `pente_glissante` — Slippery slope · low severity
Claiming that a first step will inevitably bring a cascade of extreme consequences, without demonstrating it.
**Cues:** “tomorrow they will…”, hypothetical chains presented as certain.
**Mechanism:** moves the debate from the real to an imagined future, which cannot be refuted.

### `generalisation_abusive` — Hasty generalisation · medium severity
Extending one particular case to an entire category.
**Cues:** “all of them”, “always”, “never”, a single news item turned into a social phenomenon.
**Mechanism:** the striking example is more memorable than the statistic (availability heuristic).

### `preuve_anecdotique` — Anecdotal evidence · medium severity
Substituting individual testimony for aggregate data.
**Cues:** “my aunt was cured by”, a pile of testimonies, no aggregate data at all.
**Mechanism:** an embodied story moves and convinces; it is nonetheless unverifiable and unrepresentative (survivorship bias).

### `appel_a_la_nature` — Appeal to nature · low severity
Assuming that “natural” means good and “artificial or chemical” means bad.
**Cues:** “100 % natural”, “chemical-free”, nature set against the laboratory.
**Mechanism:** an old affective heuristic: hemlock is natural, insulin is synthetic.

### `jargon_pseudo_scientifique` — Pseudo-scientific jargon · high severity
Dressing the discourse in empty or misappropriated scientific vocabulary.
**Cues:** “quantum”, “vibrational frequencies”, “cellular energy”, invented units, decorative equations, a white coat.
**Mechanism:** the appearance of science borrows its authority without its method; obscurity passes for depth.

### `epouvantail` — Straw man · medium severity
Distorting the opposing position so as to refute it easily.
**Cues:** “they claim that…” followed by a caricature, a truncated quotation, an opponent never quoted directly.
**Mechanism:** victory over the caricature is easy and spectacular; the reader often does not know the real position.

### `whataboutisme` — Whataboutism · low severity
Deflecting a criticism by pointing at another problem (“what about them?”).
**Cues:** answering an accusation with an accusation, systematically changing the subject.
**Mechanism:** refutes nothing but neutralises the criticism by symmetry; exhausts the debate.

### `deplacement_des_buts` — Moving the goalposts · low severity
Demanding ever more evidence as the previous demands are met.
**Cues:** “yes, but that does not prove that…”, criteria of proof redefined after the fact.
**Mechanism:** makes the thesis unfalsifiable; a good-faith interlocutor wears out.

### `je_pose_des_questions` — “I'm just asking questions” · medium severity
Insinuating without asserting, so as to spread a thesis without carrying the burden of proof.
**Cues:** loaded interrogative headlines (“Mere coincidence?”), “make up your own mind”, cascading insinuations.
**Mechanism:** a question steers as much as an assertion, but is legally indefensible to attack and rhetorically comfortable to hold.

### `attentes_impossibles` — Impossible expectations · medium severity
Demanding from science an absolute certainty it cannot supply, in order to discredit it.
**Cues:** “not 100 % effective, therefore useless”, “science has been wrong before”, exploitation of margins of uncertainty.
**Mechanism:** confuses residual uncertainty (normal) with total ignorance; any real knowledge becomes dismissible.

## Family D — Authority and sources

### `autorite_anonyme` — Anonymous authority · high severity
Invoking authorities that cannot be checked.
**Cues:** “scientists say”, “studies show” (with no reference), “a doctor reveals”, “according to sources”.
**Mechanism:** borrows the prestige of science or of the insider while allowing no verification at all.

### `faux_expert` — Fake expert · high severity
Presenting as an authority someone with no competence in the field concerned, or a marginal figure presented as mainstream.
**Cues:** grand titles from another field (a “doctor” of something else), an isolated expert against a consensus, a “Nobel prize” summoned outside their subject.
**Mechanism:** the aura of the title hides the absence of relevant competence (authority halo).

### `absence_de_sources` — Unsourced assertions · medium severity
Stating important facts with no possibility of verification.
**Cues:** precise figures with no origin, events reported with no link or reference, no primary source.
**Mechanism:** confidence of tone replaces evidence; the cost of checking is shifted onto the reader.

### `sources_circulaires` — Circular sources · medium severity
Sourcing from sites that quote one another or draw on the same dubious origin.
**Cues:** links only to friendly sites, “as X revealed” (X quoting Y who quoted the author), information laundering.
**Mechanism:** repetition simulates independent confirmation (illusory truth effect).

### `faux_equilibre` — False balance · medium severity
Giving equal space to a massive consensus and a marginal position, “for the sake of debate”.
**Cues:** “opinions are divided” on a settled point, one against one where reality is 99 to 1.
**Mechanism:** fairness of form manufactures doubt of substance (“there is a controversy, so nothing is certain”).

## Family E — Attacks and targets

### `ad_hominem` — Personal attack · medium severity
Discrediting the person rather than refuting the argument.
**Cues:** attacks on appearance, past, or supposed motives; demeaning nicknames; “paid to say that”.
**Mechanism:** transfers discredit from the person to the argument, without ever addressing the argument.

### `proces_d_intention` — Ascribing motives · medium severity
Attributing hidden and malicious motives without evidence.
**Cues:** “what they really want is…”, supposed motives presented as established facts.
**Mechanism:** makes the opponent indefensible: one cannot prove the absence of an intent.

### `deshumanisation` — Dehumanisation · high severity
Naming a group with vocabulary that strips it of humanity or reduces it to a threat.
**Cues:** animal, sanitary (“parasites”, “vermin”) or mechanical metaphors; an undifferentiated, threatening “them”.
**Mechanism:** lowers empathy and prepares acceptance of treatment that would otherwise be unacceptable. The highest alarm signal.

## Family F — Conflicts of interest

### `conflit_interet_commercial` — Commercial conflict of interest · high severity
Spreading an alarmist or miraculous message from which the author directly profits.
**Cues:** a “health” article ending at a shop, an affiliate link, a paid course, donations solicited through fear.
**Mechanism:** the classic business model of disinformation: fear is the funnel, the product is the exit. Follow the money.

### `solution_miracle` — Miracle cure · high severity
Promising a simple, unique and hidden remedy or solution to a complex problem.
**Cues:** “this one ingredient”, “the method they are hiding from you”, total effectiveness, no side effects, “doctors hate it”.
**Mechanism:** exploits distress and hope; the simplicity of the promise is proportional to how likely it is to be false.

## How the catalogue changes

- Adding a technique = a pull request changing this file + cases added to the [corpus](../../corpus/) + a version increment.
- The ids are **stable and final** (never renamed, since the directory references them).
- FLICC mapping: Fake experts → `faux_expert`, `autorite_anonyme` · Logical fallacies → family C · Impossible expectations → `attentes_impossibles` · Cherry picking → `cherry_picking` · Conspiracy theories → family B.
